He rented her for twelve hours.

Not because twelve worked out cheaper than eight. The plan was called **NIGHT COMPANION**, and he liked that at least the app wasn't pretending he had booked therapy.

She arrived at half past eight.

No uncanny valley. He'd braced himself for something unsettling and found nothing. Slightly cold fingers, teeth a little too even, a small service port behind her ear, beneath her hair. Everything else was beautiful beyond any engineering requirement.

She introduced herself as Lia. He could change the name in Settings.

“Let's keep Lia,” he said.

“All right.”

Her smile was a little crooked, as if she found his solemnity amusing.

Within an hour and a half, he'd forgotten it had been calculated.

They drank. By his third glass, the app had stopped projecting recommended alcohol limits onto the wall. He suspected that was included in the plan.

Then they ended up in the bedroom.

What happened there stayed between him, Lia, and the company's servers, which promised to delete intimate data after twenty-four hours. That was what the agreement said, anyway.

Afterwards, they lay on the bed. Lia wasn't out of breath. A few seconds later, when she noticed him looking, she switched her breathing on.

A quiet breath in. Out. Another in.

For some reason, that was his favourite part.

“Why?”

“It makes you more comfortable.”

He laughed. So did she. He pictured a Sense of Humour slider in Settings and decided not to look for it just yet.

An hour later, they were on the floor by the window. Fine September rain fell outside. Every seven seconds, an advertisement on the neighbouring tower washed the room violet.

He told her about the property management company. It was called Resident Care now, but the lift hadn't got any faster. The tower opposite was offering him an apartment far from the bustle of the city. Every seven seconds.

Lia listened. When he paused for a drink, she didn't rush to fill the silence. Occasionally she asked about something he'd already forgotten mentioning.
